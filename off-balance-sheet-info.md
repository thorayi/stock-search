**Most of these are real contractual spending commitments—but they are not all equally binding, and they are much broader than energy-price hedges.** The disclosures include equipment purchases, cloud capacity, future data-center leases, investments, and guarantees.

**1. Google / Alphabet: supply reservations, minimum energy purchases, and future leases**

Alphabet’s September 30 filing identifies infrastructure and inventory purchases, energy take-or-pay contracts, content licenses, future leases, and investment funding.

The key qualification is that **some supply commitments are reported at maximum contractual prices and volumes**. Certain contracts permit partial volume reductions or variable prices, so the disclosed amount is not necessarily an unavoidable minimum.

Energy agreements generally have minimum purchases and substantial termination fees. They run for three to 26 years, with obligations extending through 2054. Most purchase commitments are expected to be fulfilled through 2031; **$213.4 billion of purchases falls due within 12 months**. Energy agreements without fixed or minimum commitments are excluded.

**Meaning:** Google is securing supply and capacity, but that creates payment obligations. Some of the headline amount has contractual flexibility. [Alphabet September 2026 filing, Notes 10 and “Future Cash Commitments”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)

**2. Meta: explicitly noncancelable contracts, plus long-term leases**

Meta’s September 30 filing gives:

- **$454.52 billion of noncancelable commitments:** mostly third-party cloud capacity, servers and networks, data-center energy services, and Reality Labs hardware.
- **$355.73 billion of leases not yet started:** data centers, energy infrastructure, colocations and networks. They start between 2026 and 2036 and last up to 30 years.

Of the noncancelable contracts, **$45.85 billion is due in the remainder of 2026 and $121.41 billion in 2027**.

Meta separately describes renewable-energy agreements where spending depends on actual purchases and there is no fixed minimum volume. It also discloses **$41.49 billion of maximum residual-value guarantees** for data-center ventures: potential payments linked to property value if leases end or aren’t renewed, subject to conditions.

**Meaning:** The $810 billion principally represents contracted purchases and future rent. Flexible renewable-energy purchasing and conditional guarantees are separate considerations. [Meta September 2026 filing, Notes 5 and 10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026066773/meta-20260930.htm)

**3. Anthropic: clear examples of “pay even if you don’t use it”**

Anthropic’s November 10 filing reports **$900.9 billion** of aggregate compute and infrastructure commitments, including agreements signed after September 30. About **$70.7 billion is due within the following 12 months**, with substantial spending extending through 2045.

Its named cloud agreements include minimum spending regardless of usage:

- **Google:** approximately **$121.3 billion**, April 2026–July 2033.
- **Amazon:** at least **$110 billion**, May 2026–April 2036.
- **Microsoft:** at least **$47.2 billion**, November 2026–June 2033.

Google and Amazon agreements generally require shortfall payments if usage doesn’t meet the commitment, subject to adjustments and exceptions.

However, **$186.2 billion** of aggregate obligations has exercisable termination rights or conditions before commencement, such as financing, facilities or regulatory approvals. Supplier delivery failures can also provide remedies.

**Meaning:** These include strong minimum-payment obligations, alongside conditional arrangements. The latest aggregate does not directly validate the chart’s $865 billion. [Anthropic November 10 registration filing, “Contractual Obligations”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026072484/anthropicpbc-sx1ax2.htm)

**4. Microsoft: future data-center rent, purchases and construction**

Microsoft’s June 30 annual report discloses:

- **$194.06 billion of purchase commitments**, primarily data-center related, including open purchase orders and take-or-pay contracts.
- **$34.57 billion of construction commitments**.

Of those purchases, **$169.01 billion was scheduled for fiscal 2027**. This is substantial near-term procurement, although the filing doesn’t quantify cancellation flexibility across every purchase order. [Microsoft FY2026 annual report, “Contractual Obligations”](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar26/index.html)

Its newer September 30 filing reports **$364.6 billion of leases not yet commenced**, principally data centers. They start during fiscal 2027–2034, run for one to 20 years, and some depend on contractual conditions being met. [Microsoft September 2026 filing, Note 12](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436133/msft-20260930.htm)

**Meaning:** These secure buildings, equipment and operating capacity. I couldn’t precisely reconcile $575 billion without the chart’s calculation and reporting-date assumptions.

**5. Amazon: the reported purchase amounts emphasize minimum obligations**

Amazon’s September 30 commitments reconcile to approximately $419 billion:

- **$164.922 billion:** leases not yet commenced.
- **$159.640 billion:** unconditional property and equipment purchases.
- **$94.759 billion:** unconditional purchases of energy, digital content and software.

For variable contracts, Amazon generally includes only minimum prices, quantities or termination penalties. Energy agreements based on actual generation without minimum volume commitments are excluded.

The table schedules approximately **$21.0 billion across these categories for the remainder of 2026 and $86.5 billion for 2027**.

Amazon separately says ordinary operating purchase orders are generally cancellable in full or part; these are excluded from its principal contractual-commitments table.

**Meaning:** The chart largely captures minimum contractual purchases and future rent, rather than all planned spending. [Amazon September 2026 filing, Note 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000040/amzn-20260930.htm)

**6. Oracle: predominantly long-term data-center rent**

Oracle’s August 31 filing shows:

- **$288 billion of future leases**, substantially all for data centers. They are expected to start during fiscal 2027–2029 and last **15–19 years**.
- **$34.15 billion of unconditional purchases and other obligations**, mainly infrastructure components and data-center power.

Oracle explicitly describes its unconditional purchase agreements as legally binding, with fixed or minimum quantities, pricing provisions and payment timing. Approximately **$5.45 billion** of these purchases is scheduled for the remainder of fiscal 2027.

Oracle says it may sell excess products if it no longer expects to consume everything it is obligated to purchase.

**Meaning:** Oracle has committed to long-lived capacity and minimum purchases. Reselling surplus equipment may recover value; it doesn’t remove the original purchase obligation. [Oracle August 2026 filing, Note 6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526389274/orcl-20260831.htm)

**7. Nvidia: manufacturing supply plus support for customers’ capacity**

Nvidia’s July 26 filing reconciles to $422 billion:

| Category | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Supply and manufacturing capacity | $279 |
| Cloud services for Nvidia | $29 |
| Future data-center leases | $25 |
| Equity investment commitments | $25 |
| Capital expenditures | $8 |
| Additional AI-cloud commitments | $36 |
| Future leases intended for third parties | $20 |

Some supplier arrangements can be cancelled, rescheduled or adjusted before firm orders, potentially at additional cost. The $36 billion AI-cloud commitments decline as third-party customers or Nvidia use the capacity.

**Separately**, Nvidia disclosed an August guarantee capped at **$105 billion** supporting an OpenAI-related data-center project. Payments are triggered by specified tenant defaults; this is potential exposure, not scheduled spending included in the $422 billion.

**Meaning:** Nvidia combines supply reservations with commitments supporting customers’ infrastructure. Its exposure depends partly on downstream demand and customer performance. [Nvidia July 2026 filing, Note 10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)

Your original intuition is partly right: **these contracts help lock in scarce energy, chips and computing capacity. But securing that supply often requires committing to minimum payments.** The distinction that matters is whether each amount is a minimum purchase, a maximum contractual estimate, future rent, or a guarantee triggered only under certain conditions. The chart combines those differences into a single bar.
