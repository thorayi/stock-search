Sources and interpretation

Historical GDP: BEA annual nominal GDP via FRED (GDPA). Current GDP proxy: BEA quarterly nominal GDP via FRED (GDP). Data snapshot: November 19, 2026. Values are current U.S. dollars.

U.S. GDP is a common yardstick for every row, including Taiwan-based TSMC and U.K.-based Arm; it is not their domestic economy or total addressable market. These companies can earn revenue worldwide. Valuation is a stock of equity value, while GDP is a flow of annual economic output.

GDP cannot establish fair value. This exercise compares relative economic size. Company cash flows, margins, market share, risk and discount rates determine investment value. The supplied IPO figures are retained and are not all independently verified.

Calculation methods
  • IPO / GDP (%) = original nominal IPO valuation ÷ same-year nominal GDP × 100.
  • Required GDP = recent IPO valuation ÷ historical benchmark’s IPO/GDP ratio (as a decimal).
  • GDP multiple = required GDP ÷ current GDP proxy.
  • Required annual nominal growth = (GDP multiple)1 / years − 1.
  • The scenario holds each recent IPO’s dollar valuation fixed as GDP grows. It does not project company growth, future IPO values or stock returns. Nominal GDP growth includes inflation; it is not real GDP growth.
